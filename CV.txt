Since joining UVM full-time in July 2022, I have collaborated on short- and long-term projects with 12-15 UVM investigators, two core facilities (Bioinformatics Shared Resource and the Genomics facility), contributing to five grant submissions, five publications (one accepted, two submitted, two in preparation), five open-source software modules, 2-3 operational bioinformatics pipelines for core facilities, and one bioinformatics web platform.My recent scientific work at UVM encompasses bioinformatics and computational biology methods spanning variant analysis, epigenomics, virus typing, transcriptomics, B-cell and T-cell receptor profiling, and structural biology. The quantitative scope ranges from sample size calculations and implementation of statistical methods to sophisticated probabilistic inference and machine learning applications. My earlier work included extensive experience in sequence analysis, phylogenetics, and DNA repair enzyme modeling, utilizing mixed models, resampling techniques, and machine learning approaches.I work extensively with workflow management systems, containerized environments, version control, and high-performance computing resources as a way to enforece reproducibility and scalability. Effective communication with collaborators is central to my approach. I have also played a key role in building reproducible computational infrastructures for core facilities, dating back to the early 2000s with the creation of BioDesktop—a platform that handled thousands of orders for the UVM DNA Analysis Facility—and more recently implementing Nextflow pipelines deployed through the Seqera platform.Prior to UVM, I spent ten years in industry as a statistician at a statistical consulting firm (while working part time at UVM), where I gained extensive experience in experimental design and mixed nonlinear models while developing a deep appreciation for precision and rigor in client-facing work, which also included developing a software product for analysis of bioassay data.From a teaching and mentoring perspective, I have delivered guest lectures in the statistics and bioinformatics components of MMG courses and have mentored/supervised five students through investigator collaborations. Looking forward, I am deeply interested in artificial intelligence and eager to learn and implement and apply these methods in research.SUMMARY OF ACCOMPLISHMENTS * Integral component as the data scientist and bioinformatician member of the Bioinformatics Shared Resource and the Translational and Infectious Disesase team in collaborations with many UVM scientists, leading to multiple publications.* Part of the lead programming team for a bioinformatics web platform: BioDesktop (UVM) and for a statistical analysis web product Xymp (Precision Bioassay Inc.)* Bioinformatics key personnel member of the team in the development of a personalized antibody therapy for the treatment of cancers.
